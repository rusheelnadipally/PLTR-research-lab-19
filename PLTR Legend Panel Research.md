# PLTR Legend Panel Research

**Company:** Palantir Technologies Inc. (NASDAQ: PLTR)  
**Period:** Fiscal years ended December 31, 2021–2025  
**Units:** USD millions, except ratios and percentages

| 2024 | 2,865.507 | 467.918 | 1,153.865 | 12.634 | 1,141.231 | 507.878 | 1,246.477 | 5,094.407 | 9.18% | 17.72% | 0.24 |

Calculations:

- Revenue CAGR, 2021–2025 = `(4,475.446 / 1,541.889)^(1/4) − 1` = **30.53%**.
- Owner earnings = operating cash flow − capital expenditures.
- ROE = net income ÷ year-end total equity.

## Panel table

| Investor | Check | PLTR result |
|---|---|---|
| Buffett | ROE of 15% or more every year | **Fail** (2021–2024 were below 15%) |
| Buffett | Owner earnings positive every year | **Pass** (lowest: $183.710 million in 2022) |
| Buffett | Owner earnings grew, first year to last | **Pass** ($321.224 million to $2,100.591 million) |
| Cathie Wood | Revenue grew 15% or more a year, first year to last | **Pass** (30.53% CAGR) |
| Cathie Wood | R&D was 5% or more of revenue, latest year | **Pass** (12.46%) |
| Burry | Operating cash flow at least net income every year | **Pass** (all five years) |
| Burry | Liabilities no more than twice equity, latest year | **Pass** (0.19×) |

## Verdicts and memos

### Buffett — 2 of 3 checks pass; mixed verdict

PLTR does not meet the requirement for ROE of at least 15% in every year: ROE was -22.71% in 2021, -14.04% in 2022, 6.10% in 2023, 9.18% in 2024, and 21.83% in 2025. However, owner earnings were positive in every year and increased from $321.224 million in 2021 to $2,100.591 million in 2025. The business has become much stronger, but its five-year profitability record is not yet consistently Buffett-like.

### Cathie Wood — 2 of 2 checks pass; positive verdict

PLTR’s revenue increased from $1,541.889 million in 2021 to $4,475.446 million in 2025, a 30.53% annual growth rate, comfortably above the 15% threshold. In 2025, R&D expense was $557.677 million, equal to 12.46% of revenue, above the 5% threshold. On these two innovation-and-growth checks, PLTR fits the Cathie Wood screen.

**Threshold kept:** I keep the 15% revenue-growth threshold because it identifies genuinely high-growth companies; PLTR passes without weakening the rule.

### Burry — 2 of 2 checks pass; positive financial-quality verdict

Operating cash flow exceeded net income in every year from 2021 through 2025. In 2025, liabilities were $1,412.381 million versus equity of $7,488.011 million, a liabilities-to-equity ratio of 0.19×, far below the 2.0× limit. These checks show strong cash conversion and a conservative balance sheet, although they do not address PLTR’s market valuation.

**Unprinted-number flag across all memos:** **Clear.** Every number used above appears in the printed-facts table.

## Where the panel agrees and splits

The panel agrees that PLTR generates strong cash flow and has low balance-sheet leverage. It splits on the historical profitability record: Cathie Wood and Burry pass PLTR on their growth, innovation, cash-flow, and leverage rules, while Buffett’s panel fails the company on five-year ROE consistency even though owner earnings are strong.

## Bull and bear

**Bull case:** PLTR compounded revenue at 30.53% from 2021 to 2025 while 2025 R&D remained 12.46% of revenue. Owner earnings reached $2,100.591 million and liabilities were only 0.19× equity, showing that rapid growth is now producing substantial cash flow on a strong balance sheet.

**Bear case:** PLTR failed the 15%-ROE-every-year test, with losses in 2021 and 2022 and sub-15% ROE through 2024. The panel also ignores valuation, stock-based compensation dilution, customer concentration, and whether recent growth can persist, so strong operating results alone do not establish that the stock is attractively priced.

## Decision

I would use the panel to screen Project 2 companies **only on the condition that it is a first-pass operating-quality screen followed by a separate valuation and dilution review**.

## Reflection

Three personas can disagree while using the same facts because each converts those facts into a different decision rule: Buffett emphasizes durable profitability, Cathie Wood emphasizes innovation and growth, and Burry emphasizes cash conversion and balance-sheet safety. The threshold probe shows that a screen’s verdict is partly a design choice; changing a cutoff can change the label without changing the company’s underlying facts.
